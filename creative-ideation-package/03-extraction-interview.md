# 03 - Extraction Interview (17 questions)

This is the document you answer. Its job is to pull your ideation process out of your head and into a form Runneth can run.

**What this is actually for:** this puts how you and your team think about creative right now onto paper, in your own words, so real decisions can get made about what to do next in the ideation stage of your creative flywheel. It's not a survey for its own sake, whatever comes out of it becomes the actual foundation the engine runs on. Open with that in your own words before question 1, so the person knows why they're answering these rather than just being handed a list.

How to answer:
- Use your own words. Do not try to sound strategic.
- "Show me" beats "tell me." If you mention a doc, a folder, a saved ad, link it or paste it.
- Skip anything that genuinely does not apply. Do not invent a process you do not have.
- The section titles below are for Runneth. You never have to use that vocabulary in your answers.

## Before you start

If a Voice of Customer audit already exists for this brand, Runneth should attempt question 8 from the audit first, then confirm the answer with you rather than asking cold. Use, but verify. Never assume the audit's answer is correct just because it exists; state what it found and ask you to confirm or correct it before moving on. If no VoC audit exists yet, ask question 8 as written.

## How this gets run

This interview is run conversationally, not handed over as a form to fill in alone and not fired off one question per message either. The section headers below (Openers, Inputs, The anchor, and so on) organize the printed document, but the actual pacing groups aren't required to match them one-to-one, the qualification pair below is the one deliberate exception. Don't announce section names as a formal transition every turn ("Moving to Inputs:"); reference the structure only when it actually helps orient the person, most of the time just ask the next question(s) and keep the conversation moving.

**Questions 1 and 2 are their own pacing group: qualification.** These two, not the Openers/Inputs split in the printed document, are the exact pair the sophistication gate reads (per `04`). Ask them together as one dedicated group before anything else, wait for both answers, then stop and state the detected path out loud, addressed to the person directly and warm about it either way, per `04`'s exact wording for each path. The person can correct the detected path at any point after that. Carry the detected path forward as the tone for every question from here on: an Expert answer gets taken close to verbatim and used to override the defaults, a Partial answer gets proposed-and-adjusted framing with examples to react to, a Starting-out answer gets the most guided prompting, closer to "here's the default, does this sound right for you?" than an open-ended ask. Question 3 is not part of this pairing, it resumes normal pacing right after the gate fires.

**When more than one question goes out in the same turn, format them as clearly separate items, never run together in one paragraph.** Each question gets its own line (numbered or on its own bullet), even when two questions are being asked back to back in the same message. A person answering shouldn't have to parse two questions out of a single block of text.

**Questions with an existing-context dependency get asked with that context already in the question, not checked separately afterward.** Question 8 (One layer deeper) is the current case: it asks where the product shows up in the customer's day, which a Voice of Customer audit's pain points and trigger moments directly speak to. When an audit already exists, don't ask this cold and then cross-check the audit as a separate step. Pull what the audit already found and put it directly into how the question gets asked, for example: "Your VoC audit points to [what the audit found] as where this shows up in someone's day. Does that match, or is there more to it?" That's a single conversational turn, not a question followed by a correction pass. If no audit exists yet, ask it exactly as written, with no borrowed context to offer. Question 3 asks about product knowledge (formulation, claims, where that documentation lives), not customer language, so it never gets audit-first treatment regardless of what a VoC audit contains.

**Multi-product accounts need product attribution, not blended examples.** Check the product bible (or ask directly) before the interview starts whether this account has more than one product. If it does, never fold findings from different products into one generic illustration, that produces an ambiguous answer nobody can actually use. Before asking, know what the question is actually trying to learn (question 8 feeds the micro-moment/context-layer input per `02`, not a product-comparison exercise) and keep the question aimed only at that. Ground it in one product's clearest finding and ask it as a single, simple question, the way it was originally written, not stacked with a second product's example or a meta-question about how to pace future questions. If a later answer clearly doesn't transfer to another product, raise that as its own short follow-up when it actually comes up, don't pre-bundle it into every question on the chance it might matter.

## Openers

1. Walk me through the last really good ad idea you had. Where did it actually come from?

## Inputs

2. Where do you go to hear customers in their own words?
3. What do you need to know about the product before you can write an ad for it? Where does that live?

## The anchor

4. Do your ideas usually start from a problem the customer has, or something they want?
5. How do you decide one idea is worth building and another is not?

## Who it is for

6. How do you decide who a specific ad is for?
7. Give an example of two customers who buy the same product for completely different reasons.

## One layer deeper

8. Where does the product actually show up in your best customer's day?

## The message

9. Once you know who it's for, how do you decide what the ad actually says?
10. Think of your best ad ever. Underneath the words, what was it really saying?

## Awareness

11. Does the same ad idea get written differently depending on where the viewer sits on that awareness stage (unaware, solution aware, product aware)? How does the wording or approach change?

## How it looks

12. How do you pick what an ad looks like? What are your go-to formats?
13. When you save an ad you see in the wild, what made you save it?

## The moves

14. How do you decide what to do next after the hook to actually hold attention?

## Output and loop

15. When is a concept done for you? What does it have to include before it goes to a brief?
16. After ads run, what do you do with the results? How does a winner change what you make next?

## Closers

17. What part of your process would you never skip, and what do you quietly skip when you're rushed?
