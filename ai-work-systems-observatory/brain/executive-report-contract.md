# Executive report contract

## Product promise

Explain how the organization works today, which recurring AI systems are actually operating, and how a better operating model would change roles, decisions, and weekly cadence.

## Mandatory chapters

1. **Executive thesis**: the core operating-model diagnosis, the Monday change, and no more than four supporting metrics.
2. **How the team operates today**: roles, responsibilities, recurring decisions, meetings and rhythms, handoffs, manual coordination, reviewed outputs, and approval gates.
3. **Current operating systems**: qualified end-to-end loops only. Show trigger, inputs, AI work, output, audience, human control, consumption, health window, owner, and evidence.
4. **Alternative operating model**: three to five labeled proposed systems. Show the gap, trigger, inputs, AI responsibility, human judgment, output, destination, owner recommendation, success signal, and evidence.
5. **Role and rhythm changes**: stop, continue, start by role, plus the weekly or monthly cadence after the proposed systems exist.
6. **Opportunity portfolio and build sequence**: top three opportunities, readiness, value hypothesis, next move, and a phased roadmap.
7. **Evidence and governance appendix**: source coverage, fact/inference/recommendation ledger, confidence, limitations, unknowns, controls, privacy, and prohibited claims.

## Synthesis questions

The strategist pass must answer:

- Where does manual coordination remain?
- Which decisions can be prepared automatically?
- Which outputs can remain continuously updated?
- Where is human judgment mandatory?
- Which next system unlocks the most capacity?
- How does the team's week change if it exists?

## Regression checklist

A report is incomplete unless it answers, in visible prose:

1. What changes on Monday?
2. Which roles work differently?
3. What does AI do?
4. What do humans approve?
5. What are the three highest-value systems to build?
6. What evidence supports those recommendations?

## Rendering rules

- Lead with an editorial thesis, not KPIs.
- Use a role and responsibility map, end-to-end system loops, current-versus-proposed comparisons, system-loop cards, cadence timeline, opportunity portfolio, roadmap, and collapsible evidence appendix.
- Keep observed state and recommendations in separately labeled sections.
- Every current claim has evidence. Every recommendation is labeled and points to evidence or a hypothesis.
- Missing evidence remains unknown, never zero.
